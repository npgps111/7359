Be careful !

You were about to share a simulated news story.

This article was created for media-literacy education by using AI. AI is an effective tool for creating fake news — it can generate realistic text, images, audio and video at scale, and it can imitate real outlets, people and events.

  1. 01

    AI can fabricate realistic content

    Text, images, audio and video are cheap to generate. A convincing clip is not proof.

  2. 02

    AI can imitate real outlets

    Names, logos and URLs can be copied. Check the address and the publisher’s own site.

  3. 03

    AI can target your emotions

    Fear, anger and urgency make people share before checking. Notice the feeling and slow down.

  4. 04

    Real media can be edited or reused

    Photos and video can be altered or placed in a false context. Compare the image with the place and date.

  5. 05

    Faces and voices can be misused

    Deepfakes can copy a real face or voice. Protect your own images and verify clips with the person or organisation.

Guidance this page draws on: EDB Information Literacy Framework (2024) · EDB Smart Tips for Spotting Fake News · OECD Counter-Disinformation Toolkit · PCPD AI Deepfake Guidance.

    Before you share

Stop · Verify · Think

01

Stop

Pause before reacting or forwarding. Strong emotion and urgency can make an unverified claim feel credible.

02

Verify

Find the original report, announcement or study. Check the organisation's official channels and compare with trusted news sources.

03

Think

Ask who made it, what evidence is shown and who benefits. Can you find the same claim online on a reliable site? If not, don't share.
