Be careful !

You were about to share a simulated news story.

This article was created for media-literacy education by using AI. AI is an effective tool for creating fake news — it can generate realistic text, images, audio and video at scale, and it can imitate real outlets, people and events.

  1. 01

    AI can fabricate realistic content

    Text, photos, audio and video can be generated quickly. A convincing image or voice clip is no longer proof that something happened.

  2. 02

    AI can imitate real outlets

    Names, logos and web addresses can be copied or slightly altered. Check the address and the publisher’s own website, not just the look of the page.

  3. 03

    AI can target your emotions

    Fake stories are often designed to trigger fear, anger or urgency so that people share before they check. Notice the feeling and slow down.

  4. 04

    Real media can be edited or reused

    AI tools can alter photos and video, and real footage can be placed in a false context. Compare the image with the place, date and event.

  5. 05

    Faces and voices can be misused

    Deepfakes can copy a real person’s face or voice. Protect your own images and data, and verify a clip with the person or organisation before sharing it.

Stop · Verify · Think

01

Stop

Pause before reacting or forwarding. Strong emotion and urgency can make an unverified claim feel more credible than it is.

02

Verify

Find the original report, announcement, or study. Check the named organisation's official channels and compare the claim with trusted news sources.

03

Think

Ask who created the message, what evidence is actually shown, what may be missing, and who benefits if people believe or share it.
